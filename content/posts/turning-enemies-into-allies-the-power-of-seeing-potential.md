***Did you know that embracing your enemy can bring you power and knowledge?*** 

Turning an enemy into a friend can sometimes benefit you more than a relationship with someone who already loves you. This is what Jesus teaches us in the [Bible](https://everrockwrite.netlify.app/posts/understanding-divine-emergence/): we should pray for our enemies and extend kindness to them, despite the pain they may have caused us (Matthew 5:44). 

We should not fight them or treat them as enemies. Imagine a king who embraced a man who had killed his own father-in-law. He did so not because he lacked resentment, but because he believed everyone has a talent that can be used for the common good. 

The same principle appears when Jesus called **Saul**, later known as **Paul**, on the road to **Damascus**. 

Saul was persecuting believers, yet Jesus turned him into one of the greatest preachers of the gospel. Jesus saw positive potential in Saul where others saw only a threat. 

Today, many of us as Christians believe that once we identify an enemy, we must fight them right away. In doing so, we become our own enemies and neglect the teaching of Jesus. We should look for positive potential rather than dwell on the negative and criticise. 

Please do not misunderstand me: I am not saying we should stop praying against Satan and his agents, for Scripture reminds us that our real struggle is not against flesh and blood **(Ephesians 6:12)**. 

The king in this story saw potential in his enemy. Rather than punish or destroy him, he put him to good use, and it gave the king greater wisdom and strength. Indeed, **GREAT KING SEJONG.**
