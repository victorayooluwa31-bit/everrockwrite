That evening, Daniel returned home to find Mr. Adeyemi waiting for him.

The old man had been unwell for several weeks, and his condition had recently worsened.

"Daniel, come and sit," he said.

Daniel pulled a chair closer.

"Sir, you should be resting."

"I know. But there are things I want to tell you."

Mr. Adeyemi reached for a small envelope on the table.

"Inside this envelope is a letter. I have decided to transfer a portion of my business interests to you. I want you to continue developing what we started."

Daniel was speechless.

"Sir, I don't know what to say."

"Then don't say anything. Just promise me something."

"Anything, sir."

"Never allow your success to make you forget the people who stood beside you when you had nothing. And when you meet someone who is going through what you went through, don't just give them advice. Give them an opportunity if you can."

Daniel's eyes filled with tears.

A few weeks later, Mr. Adeyemi passed away.

At his funeral, Daniel stood before the gathering and struggled to speak.

He remembered the day he had met the old man beneath the mango tree. He remembered the sack, the conversation, and the opportunity that had changed his life.

When he finally spoke, he said:

"Many people knew me when I was successful, but very few recognised me when I was broken. Mr. Adeyemi did. He didn't see a failed businessman. He saw a man who still had something to offer.

"I once believed that losing my business meant losing my future. I thought betrayal had destroyed my destiny. I thought the woman who left me had taken away my happiness.

"But today, I understand something. If I had never lost that business, I might never have met the man who gave me a second chance. If I had never experienced betrayal, I might never have learned the value of discernment. If I had never been rejected, I might never have discovered the strength to build again.

"I am not saying that pain is pleasant. I am not saying that failure should be celebrated. Some experiences leave scars that take years to heal.

"But I have learned that a painful chapter does not have to become the final chapter of your story."

He paused, looked towards the sky, and continued.

"To anyone sitting in a dark room today, wondering whether life has anything left to offer, I want you to remember this: Don't bury yourself because life has buried your plans. A seed is buried to grow, not to disappear.

"You may not understand what is happening today. You may not see the benefit of your struggles. But don't allow temporary circumstances to convince you that your story is over.

"Keep learning. Keep working. Keep believing. And when another opportunity comes, be prepared to recognise it."
