Leadership is not an easy task, and that is why the **Apostle Paul** urges us to pray for those in authority **(1 Timothy 2:1-2)**. Jesus also taught us to pray for others and to honors them, because leaders are human beings, and our prayers help keep them from leading us astray. 

Many of the problems we face in Nigeria and everywhere else, whether in our homes, workplaces, states, nations, ministries, or churches, can be traced partly to us as followers. Instead of praying for our leaders, we choose to blame them. 

This habit begins at home, and the Yoruba proverb “*atile la ti n keso rode*” reminds us that what happens outside begins in the home. 

Your husband can become a better man when you pray for him daily. Our pastors can grow stronger in their ministry when we cover them in prayer. Our leaders in the state and the nation can guide us in the right direction when we commit to praying for them. Even a boss who withholds appreciation for your good work can change when you choose to pray for him or her. 

The Bible says, “**The effective, fervent prayer of a righteous man avails much” (James 5:16)**. 

Our leaders are doing their best, so rather than blaming them, let us pray that they will lead with wisdom and do what is truly good for us. Our society will be better if we pray for our leaders, and we must remember that everyone is a leader in one way or another.

So, **STOP BLAMING THE GOVERNMENT AND START PRAYING FOR THEM.** 

Prayer can change hearts in a way that blame never will. God will help us!
