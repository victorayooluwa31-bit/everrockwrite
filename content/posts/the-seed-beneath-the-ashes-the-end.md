Five years later, Daniel established a foundation that provided training, mentorship, and small business opportunities for young entrepreneurs who had experienced business failure.

At the opening ceremony, he invited young people from different communities.

One of them was a young man named Samuel, who had recently lost his savings in a failed business.

After the programme, Samuel approached him.

"Sir, I don't know if I can ever recover from what happened to me. I've lost everything."

Daniel looked at the young man and saw a reflection of his former self.

He smiled, placed a hand on his shoulder, and said:

"Samuel, I won't promise you that everything will become easy. I won't tell you that you won't fail again. But I will tell you this: your present situation is not a reliable prediction of your future."

He handed Samuel an application form.

"Come back on Monday. Let's see what we can build together."

Samuel collected the form with trembling hands.

As Daniel watched him walk away, he remembered the old man beneath the mango tree.

He finally understood that the greatest reward of his journey was not the business he had built, the money he had recovered, or the recognition he had received.

It was the person he had become through everything he had endured.

He had entered the season of failure believing that life had taken something away from him.

He left it understanding that life had also planted something within him.

And sometimes, the greatest harvest begins in the darkest soil.

**"Every adversity, every failure, every heartache carries with it the seed of an equal or greater benefit. Napoleon Hill"**
