There was once a young boy named Daniel who grew up in a small village. While other children spent their evenings playing by the riverside, Daniel would sit under a dim lantern reading old, worn-out books his father kept in a wooden box.

The villagers mocked him. “*Why waste your time with papers? Go and learn how to trade goats or farm like the rest of us!”* they laughed.

But Daniel kept reading. He read about kings who ruled wisely, inventors who changed the world, and leaders who inspired nations.

Years passed. One day, the village faced a terrible drought. The farms dried up, animals died, and everyone panicked. But Daniel had read about irrigation systems in a book. He gathered the villagers and explained how they could redirect water from the nearby stream to their farmlands. Skeptical at first, they reluctantly followed his plan. Within weeks, green crops began to grow again.

The same villagers who once mocked him now called him Leader.

### **The Power of Reading**

Reading is not just about gaining knowledge it’s about expanding your mind. Every book you read is a conversation with great minds, both living and dead. 

> ***Leaders are not born; they are shaped by wisdom, discipline, and vision and much of that comes through reading.***

A reader sees farther than others because books open hidden doors of possibility.

A reader speaks with confidence because knowledge fuels courage.

A reader makes better decisions because they are guided by principles, not just emotions.

#### **Illustration**

Think of your mind as a bank account. Every time you read, you are depositing **wisdom, ideas,** and **strategies**. When challenges come like drought in Daniel’s story you withdraw from that account. But if you never read, your account will be empty, and when life demands solutions, you’ll have nothing to offer.

### **Lesson**

***Readers become leaders because they see what others cannot see.*** They learn from the mistakes of others without having to make the same mistakes themselves. They find solutions when others see problems.

So, the next time you open a book, remember: you’re not just reading you’re preparing yourself for leadership. 

> ***Leaders don’t wait for opportunities; they create them. And the fuel for creation is knowledge.***

If you want to lead tomorrow, read today.

Because **readers are leaders**.
