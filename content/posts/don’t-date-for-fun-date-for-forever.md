***What is the point of dating when marriage is not in your mind?***

I understand that not everyone you date will become your husband or wife. But before you enter any relationship, you should first have it in your heart that you are looking at this person as someone you could marry. That is the difference between dating and playing.

For me, ***a relationship that will not end in marriage is a waste of time***. Why would I spend my days, my money and my emotions on something with no good ending? Meanwhile, the person God has for me is somewhere out there, looking for me too. If I am busy being **“faithful and loyal”** to someone I will never marry, I am not being loyal at all. I am only delaying both of us.

This is one of the quiet problems in our society. Marrying the wrong person is one of the biggest mistakes a person can make. I have seen young people with big dreams and real talent die early because of domestic violence. I have seen lives ruined because of one partner. When that happens, it does not stop with the two people. It affects their families and their community.

I recently came across a post on Facebook. A guy proposed to his girlfriend, and she turned him down. I do not know her reasons, and I am not here to judge her. But the story made me ask a simple question: ***why enter a relationship you know will not end well, and why stay in one where you cannot see yourself in the future?***

Think of a different story. Imagine a young man called **[Tunde](https://everrockwrite.netlify.app/posts/who-can-please-the-word/)** and a young woman called **Bisi**. They were together for **four years**. Everyone called them ***“the perfect couple”***. But Tunde had never once talked about marriage, and Bisi was afraid to ask. She kept telling herself, “He will talk about it when he is ready.”

Four years later, Tunde said, “I love you, but I am not ready for marriage. I may never be.” Bisi cried for weeks. She was no longer 22, her friends were getting married, and she had turned down two good men while waiting. Tunde moved on without much trouble, but Bisi was the one who had lost years.

Nobody was a villain in that story. Two people simply never had the one conversation that mattered, and one of them paid for it.

#### **Advice for young, single and dating people**

* **Know what you want before you start.** If you are not ready for marriage, say so honestly. Do not let someone give you years of love that you cannot repay.
* **Talk about the future early.** Within the first few months, ask: *“Where do you see this going?*” A person who avoids the question has already given you part of the answer.
* **Do not stay because of the time you have already spent.** Four years in the wrong place is not a reason for a fifth. Leaving early costs less than leaving late.
* **Look at character, not only feelings.** Watch how they handle anger, money, family and being told no. Love without character can turn into pain.
* **Do not ignore warning signs.** Violence, control and disrespect do not “go away after marriage”. They get worse.
* **Pray, but also use your head.** Ask God for the right person, and then use the sense He gave you to see whether this one fits.
* **Be someone’s right person too.** Do not only look for a good partner. Become one.

Dating is not a crime, and ***not every relationship has to end in marriage***. But your time, your heart and your future are too valuable to spend on a relationship that you know is going nowhere.

So before you say “**yes**”, and before you say “**I love you**”, ask yourself honestly: **am I building something, or am I only passing time?**
