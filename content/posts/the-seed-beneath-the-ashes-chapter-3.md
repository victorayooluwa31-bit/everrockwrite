The following morning, Daniel visited Mr. Adeyemi.

He was taken to a modest farm on the outskirts of town. There were poultry houses, fish ponds, vegetable gardens, and a small processing facility.

Mr. Adeyemi explained that he had been looking for someone to help manage the farm's supply and distribution operations.

"I don't have the energy to supervise everything myself anymore," he said. "I need someone who understands business, knows how to communicate with customers, and is willing to work."

Daniel was surprised.

"But sir, I have no money to invest."

"I didn't ask you for money. I asked whether you could work."

Daniel accepted immediately.

The salary was modest, but the opportunity was enough to give him a reason to wake up every morning.

For the first few months, he worked harder than anyone expected. He reorganised the farm's records, improved communication with customers, and developed a simple distribution system that reduced delays.

One day, he noticed something unusual.

The farm was throwing away large quantities of agricultural by-products that could have been processed into useful animal feed ingredients.

He approached Mr. Adeyemi with an idea.

"Sir, what if we could turn some of these discarded materials into affordable feed supplements for small-scale poultry farmers?"

Mr. Adeyemi looked at him.

"Have you studied the idea?"

"Not fully, sir. But I believe it is worth investigating."

"Then investigate it."

Daniel spent weeks researching, consulting agricultural experts, and conducting small trials. He made mistakes, adjusted his approach, and started again.

Eventually, he developed a practical processing method that helped the farm reduce waste and create an additional source of income.

Mr. Adeyemi was impressed.

"You see, Daniel," he said one afternoon, "when you first came here, you thought you had lost everything. But you came with something money could not buy."

"What was that, sir?"

"Experience. You had learned how to build a business, how to handle customers, and unfortunately, how to recognise the consequences of misplaced trust. You were looking at your losses, but I was looking at what those losses had taught you."

Daniel smiled.

For the first time in months, he felt that his life was moving somewhere.
