The other day, I saw a question written on the back of a bus: ***“Who can please the world?”***

It stopped me, because the answer is simple: nobody.

It reminded me of a young man I know, let’s call him Tunde, who learned this the hard way. Let me tell you his story.

Tunde had a father’s sister who loved him dearly. Whenever he visited her, she and her sons welcomed him like one of their own. They fed him, made room for him, and treated him with so much warmth that he felt at home. On one of his earlier visits, one of his cousins even surprised him with his first Android phone, after years of using a button phone.

So after finishing his secondary school exams, Tunde visited them again. He planned to stay a week, but he still had one more exam to write, so he had to leave. His aunt and cousins wanted him to stay longer. When he promised to come back, they didn’t believe him. They said others had made the same promise and never returned.

But Tunde was a man of his word. When the exams were over, he went back.

His mother told him not to go. But he wanted to keep his promise and make the people who had been so good to him happy, so he ignored her.

At first, things were fine. His aunt and cousins even helped him find a job. Then, during the festive season, a disagreement broke out between his father, his mother, and his aunt, and it got heated. His mother told him to pack his things and leave for good.

Tunde hesitated. He didn’t want his aunt to think his mother was controlling him. He also didn’t want to leave with a fight, because he believed opportunities could come from their side in the future, and he didn’t want to ruin the relationship. So he stayed.

Days into the new year, he lost his job when his boss refused to pay his full salary. He was still processing his university admission at the time, and with no job and nothing to do, the days felt empty. There was another problem. His aunt wanted him to live with her, and he didn’t want that.

Then his mother called. She had found him a job back home, one that even related to the course he was about to study.

Tunde thought his aunt would understand when he told her. Instead, she was angry, and so were her sons. The job had come through his mother, so they believed he was lying just to go back to her. To them, it meant he couldn’t think for himself and that his mother was controlling him. The next day, they sent him back home.

The very thing Tunde had tried to avoid had happened. He had wanted to settle the matter between everyone and build a true relationship with his aunt, but it ended in the exact conflict he feared.

He arrived home broke. His mother didn’t scold him. She comforted him and spoke wisdom into him. That was when it hit him: all along, he had been trying to please his father, his mother, and his aunt just so peace could reign, and he was wrong. In trying to please everybody, he ignored the one person who had warned him out of love, and he pleased none of them.

In time, the hurt faded. Tunde understood that his aunt was not a bad person. She was human. She loved him, she was caught in a family conflict, and she reacted the way people do when they feel they are losing someone. He never forgot the kindness she and her sons showed him.

Here is what he learned:

* **You cannot please everybody.** Even good people who love you can be disappointed when your choices don’t match their expectations.
* **Peace is not built by pleasing everyone.** Trying to keep every side happy can bring the very conflict you want to avoid.
* **Listen to those who truly have your interest at heart.** Their warnings may be inconvenient, but they often save you.
* **Don’t turn people into villains.** Someone can be generous to you and still hurt you in a moment of emotion. Remember the good.

**Dear reader, don’t spend your life chasing everyone’s approval. You will end up exhausted, and someone will still be unhappy. Do what is right, stay focused on your goals, and seek to please God first.**
