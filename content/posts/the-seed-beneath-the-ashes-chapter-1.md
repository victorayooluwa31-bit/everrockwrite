The rain had stopped, but the tears in Daniel's eyes had not.

He sat on the bare floor of his father's unfinished house, staring at a letter that had destroyed everything he had spent five years building.

It was a rejection letter from the bank.

His business loan had been declined.

For three years, Daniel had worked tirelessly to build a small agricultural supply business. He had started with nothing but a borrowed motorcycle, a second-hand phone, and a dream that one day his family would never have to struggle for money again.

At first, things went well. Customers came, orders increased, and he gradually became known among farmers in his community.

Then he made one mistake.

He trusted the wrong person.

His childhood friend, Michael, had convinced him to invest almost all his savings in a supposedly profitable poultry project. Michael promised that within six months, they would double their investment.

Daniel hesitated, but friendship clouded his judgment.

He eventually agreed.

Two months later, Michael disappeared with the money.

The business collapsed shortly afterward. Suppliers demanded payment, customers lost confidence, and Daniel's workers had to be dismissed.

The same people who once praised his determination now whispered that he had failed.

Some called him foolish. Others said he had been too ambitious.

Even his fiancée, Grace, began to question their future.

One evening, she visited him.

"Daniel, we need to talk," she said, sitting beside him.

He already knew what was coming.

"I cannot continue like this," she began. "I have waited patiently, but nothing seems to be changing. My parents are asking questions. My friends are getting married. I don't even know what to tell them anymore."

Daniel looked at her.

"So what are you saying?"

Grace lowered her head.

"I think we should take a break."

He remained silent for a moment before asking, "If I become successful tomorrow, will you still need that break?"

"That's not fair, Daniel."

"No, Grace. What isn't fair is judging my entire future by my present condition."

She stood up, picked up her handbag, and walked towards the door.

Before leaving, she turned around.

"I hope things work out for you."

Daniel smiled painfully.

"So do I."

The door closed.

And for the first time in many years, he felt completely alone.
