Three weeks passed.

Daniel struggled to find something meaningful to do. His phone no longer rang with business enquiries. The people who once requested his services had moved on to other suppliers.

He began taking small jobs wherever he could find them.

One afternoon, while returning from a construction site where he had worked as a labourer, he met an old man sitting beneath a mango tree.

The man was struggling to lift a heavy sack.

Daniel immediately dropped his bag and helped him.

"Thank you, young man," the old man said.

"You're welcome, sir."

The old man studied his face.

"You look troubled."

Daniel forced a smile.

"I'm fine, sir."

The old man laughed gently.

"Young man, your mouth says you are fine, but your eyes are telling a different story."

Daniel hesitated before explaining everything: the business, the betrayal, the debts, and the woman who had left him.

The old man listened without interrupting.

When Daniel finished, the man asked a question.

"Tell me something. When a farmer plants a seed, what happens to it?"

"It grows into a plant, sir."

"Not immediately. First, it is buried beneath the soil. It loses its visibility. Nobody celebrates it. Nobody sees what is happening to it. Sometimes, the soil becomes hard, and the rain refuses to come. Yet something is happening underneath."

Daniel nodded.

The old man continued.

"Perhaps your life is not ending. Perhaps you are simply in a season where your growth is happening beneath the surface."

Daniel looked at him thoughtfully.

"That sounds encouraging, sir, but encouragement doesn't pay debts."

The old man laughed.

"True. But a discouraged man rarely finds the strength to do what will pay those debts."

He introduced himself as Mr. Adeyemi, a retired agricultural entrepreneur who had spent most of his life working with farmers.

Before they parted, he gave Daniel his phone number.

"Come and see me tomorrow. I may have something for you."
